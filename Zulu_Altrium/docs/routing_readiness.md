## THE ANSWER

**NO — not yet, but you are close, and the placement does not have to change.**

The single reason: the board's *geometry* is ready and the *setup* is not. The one rule that governs every trace you are about to draw — the single board-wide Width rule — has a preferred width of 3.937 mil (0.09999 mm), and the gap between two adjacent CPG236 lands is 0.500 − 0.225044 = 0.274956 mm, which at the file's own 0.09 mm clearance admits a maximum of 0.094956 mm of copper. Alongside that sit four more things that are free to fix now and expensive to discover later (a missing keep-out the repo's own docs call a must-fix, live keep-out art in the library that 144 components would pull back onto copper, an undecided net, and zero net classes / no differential-pair object). None of them is a placement problem. All of them are a day's work.

## WHICH CONCLUSIONS CHANGED UNDER CHALLENGE

Six assessments, eleven challenges. Nine challenges stood, and these are the conclusions that moved:

**The re-pin of D14/D15 is dead.** The strip assessment wanted D14@A14 and D15@A15 moved. The challenger showed the fallback balls D1/D2/B2/A2 are MGTPTXN0/TXP0/TXP1/TXN1 of bank 216 — GTP transceiver outputs, not I/O — and that `docs/repin_study.json` carries `"rotation": 90` while `tools/placement_report.txt` line 18 has U1 on the board at rotation 0, so every cycle in that study was solved for an orientation the board does not have; re-scored at rotation 0 the proposed moves make both nets *longer* (+2.50 mm each). The corridor north of U1 is 0.7025–0.7275 mm, which carries exactly 3 lanes with ~0.11 mm spare, and exactly 3 row-A signals need it. **No schematic change. Route them as three parallel Top lanes.**

**"The U3 premise in the brief is wrong" is itself wrong.** The congestion assessment claimed the old board's U3 sat south-east of U1 and that the geometry had therefore changed. The challenger traced that to an EAGLE MR90 transform bug (it put U3 at y −5.54, off a board that spans 0–25.4) and showed old U3 at x 17.975..38.775 against new U3 at x 17.950..38.750 — **the same place, to a third of a millimetre**, with a byte-for-byte identical pin-out on all 230 netted balls. This is the single most important reversal in the set and it drives the section below.

**The via keep-out in the U2/U1 corridor is out; a spread rule is in.** Both the strip and congestion assessments wanted vias banned in the corridor. The challenger measured 100 legal through-via slots there over y 9.71..17.49 and showed that 15 of U1's west-bound nets sit on rings 0 and 1 and are geometrically barred from the annulus (a ring-3 via clears a ring-2 land by 0.2375 mm, against the 0.2562 mm a 3 mil trace needs — once ring 3 is populated nothing passes through it). Ban the vias and those 15 nets must travel north–south inside a 13-lane throat, 10 of them at the same y. **The old board did not fail because vias existed in the corridor; it failed because 15–17 of 24 sat on two x values.**

**Plane clearance stays at 0.25 mm.** The rules assessment wanted 0.25 → 0.15. The challenger showed that at 0.15 the orthogonal web between adjacent voids on the 0.5 mm grid is exactly 0.000 mm — tangent circles, not a plane — and that 0.15 is below JLC's 0.20 mm hole-to-inner-copper floor, so CAM would enlarge it back and leave you worse than you started.

**PlaneConnect "Direct for vias" does not save the plane under U1.** The power assessment sold it as the fix for the erased GND planes. The challenger's point is decisive: every *signal* escape via is non-GND and is governed by PlaneClearance, which that recommendation never touches. 35+ escape vias × 0.385 mm² in a 27 mm² annulus perforates both planes regardless. Direct-for-vias is still worth doing for board-wide GND stitching; it is not a plane rescue.

**The plane pullback change is a no-op, and the two dead-copper islands have a different cause than the two starved thermals.** Layers 39 and 40 each carry four *netless* 1.016 mm tracks on the board outline — negative artwork that pins the copper edge 0.508 mm inboard no matter what PLANEnPULLBACK says. And the dead copper is not at X2-20 at all: it is a ~0.124 mm² crumb in the **south-west** corner behind X2-40 (ANALOG-IO1, a non-GND pin whose 0.758 mm void traps it). One edit — moving the artwork edge to 0.250 mm inset — clears all four violations; the X2-GND rule clears two.

**Two places where I overrule the challenger, on my own measurement.** I parsed Pads6 directly this session:

- **U1's 238 lands are ROUND** (ShapeTop = 1 for all 238), 0.225044 mm diameter, x 41.900..50.900, y 7.400..16.400. The fanout challenger refuted the interstitial dog-bone on the premise that the lands are square, computing 0.04434 mm at a four-land centre. With round lands it is 0.353553 − 0.1125 − 0.150 = **0.091053 mm against a 0.090 mm rule** — legal, by 1.05 µm. So the dog-bone is not impossible. But 1 µm is a DRC pass, not a manufacturing margin, and the practical conclusion is the challenger's anyway: **do not build the escape on interstitial vias.**
- **X2's pads are 1.524 mm on 1.016 mm holes** (40 pads, 39 round + 1 rect, x 1.27..59.69, y 1.27..24.13). The strip challenger claimed a ~2.43–2.53 mm pad OD and concluded both GND planes are *already severed* along both header rows. That figure is the relief-inflated bounding box (hole/2 + 0.508 expansion = 1.266 mm half-extent), not the pad. Real anti-pad at 0.25 mm clearance is 1.516 mm on a 2.54 mm pitch: **a 1.024 mm continuous copper web.** The planes are not severed. Drop that item.

## THE U3–U1 CROSSING — THE DECISIVE ITEM

**The placement does not have to change. Do not move U3, do not rotate it, do not widen the strip.** But you are standing on exactly the same ground as the board that died, with two fewer layers, and the thing that saves you is a fan-out you place before the router does.

**What is measured, and it is uncomfortable.** U3 (Bottom, TSOP-II-54, rot 90) occupies x 17.950..38.750 with two east-west pad rows at y 6.170 and y 17.530; east copper edge 38.975. U1's west land edge is 41.7875. Gap **2.8125 mm**. On the board that stalled at 26 airwires, U3 sat at x 17.975..38.775 and the gap was 2.8875 mm. That is the same placement, not the same kind of placement — U3 moved 0.025 mm, U1 0.100 mm. All 230 netted CPG236 balls carry the identical net. The 39 shared signals have the identical ring histogram (16/13/10) and the identical escape-face split (24 west, 13 south, 2 north). The old via wall is named in the old file: of the vias in the x 38.75–39.25 bin, 15–17 sit on x 39.20/39.24 spanning y 6.344..14.744 — A0, A2, A3, A7, A11, A12, CKE, SDRAM-CLK, D8, D11 — the SDRAM bus, crossing the core in one column.

**And two things got worse, measured.** The FT2232 went from a QFN64 (east copper 38.208) to an LQFP64 (east copper 39.425), so the north-south corridor west of U1 fell from 3.680 mm to **2.3625 mm**; and routing layers fell from six to four. North-south capacity at the exact x where the old router built its wall: 21 lanes × 6 = 126 then, 13 lanes × 4 = 52 now. That is a 59% cut, and it is the only budget number on this board that regressed.

**So why is the answer still "don't move anything"?** Because the crossing was never capacity-limited, and it is not now.

- 91 nets have pads on both sides of the cut at x = 40.385. Parallel trace lanes at 0.0762/0.09: L3 127, L4 127, Bottom 98, Top 61 = **413 lanes. 22% utilisation.** (Top is 61, not the 95 first claimed, because U2's east pad row leaves 0.2201 mm between lands against the 0.2562 mm a 3 mil trace needs — nothing crosses Top over y 9.71..17.49.)
- The corridor holds **100 legal through-via slots** over y 9.71..17.49, in five columns at 0.39 mm pitch from x 39.665 to 41.5475. Roughly 18 vias are actually forced into it (16 ring-0/ring-1 west-face escapes plus AIN15_N and AIN15_P).
- The old board stalled while occupying **12.3%** of its theoretical per-layer packing. Density never killed it.
- U1's rings 3–5 are completely empty: 120 via slots on a 0.5 mm grid, every one of which clears hole-to-hole (0.30 mm edge against a 0.20 mm rule). That annulus existed on the board that failed too. It was never the missing resource; it was the unused one.
- U3's inboard pocket — Bottom layer, between the pad rows, x 17.95..38.75, y 6.860..16.840, **9.98 × 20.8 mm** — is completely empty of copper. That is where the layer changes and the row-sorting belong, 10–20 mm west of the strip.
- One genuine improvement: both internal planes are GND. The old board had **zero** polygons; GND and VCC3V3 were routed as traces, 894 + 899 wire segments and 220 of its 860 vias, 19 of them inside x 38.0..41.5. All of that is gone from this corridor.

**The one place 2.8125 mm actually bites, measured:** if the north-row/south-row sort is done *inside* the strip, 17 west-face nets need 17 × 0.1662 − 0.09 = 2.735 mm of vertical trace width and the strip offers 2.6325 mm — 16 lanes. It fails by one lane, on one layer. Do the sort in U3's pocket (124 vertical lanes per layer) and the problem does not exist.

**So the mechanism that killed the last board is via discipline, not geometry, and the cure is a rule, not a move.** Concretely: fan-out vias in x 39.665..41.5475 must use all five columns, with no more than four vias sharing a column within any 2 mm of y. The same 24 vias then occupy 1.95 mm of the corridor's 7.78 mm height instead of 9.36 mm. Do **not** impose a blanket via keep-out there — 15 of the 24 west-bound nets are on rings 0/1, have no annulus route, and must change layer in that corridor.

**The one placement change worth pricing, and it is not U3.** Shift LD0–LD5 1.3 mm west (BTN leaves 1.650 mm of slack: LD west edge 23.350, BTN east edge 21.700) and U2 1.6 mm west, restoring the corridor from 2.3625 mm to ~3.96 mm — 22 lanes per layer instead of 13, and nine via columns instead of five. Cost: seven top-side parts plus U2, plus U2's bottom-side decoupling following it, plus a fresh placement verification — roughly half a day. It is **not required by any measured demand figure**; north-south demand in that corridor cannot be measured until something is routed. But it is the only measured regression against the board that failed, in the exact place that board failed, on two fewer layers. **My recommendation: do it now.** Half a day before the first track is much cheaper than unpicking ~6,000 mm of copper, and if you defer it you are betting the attempt on the one number that got worse.

**Priced and rejected:** moving U3 west 3 mm widens the gap to 5.81 mm and adds ~60 mm to the south-row nets, solving a problem the measurements say does not exist. Rotating U3 180° moves D0–D7 from x 29.15..37.95 to x 19.55..28.35, adding ~150 mm to the bus. Moving U2 alone without moving the LEDs is capped at 0.225 mm by LD5's land at x 25.900 against the 0.30 mm part-to-part clearance `place_board.py` enforces — it buys one Top lane.

## SET UP BEFORE ROUTING

**Width.** There is exactly one Width rule: scope All, priority 1, MIN 3 mil, PREFERRED 3.937 mil, MAX 19.685 mil. Change PREFERRED to 3 mil (or set Preferences → PCB Editor → Interactive Routing width source to *Rule Minimum*). If you want a scoped escape rule instead, **place a Room over U1 first** — Rules6 contains no RoomDefinition record, and `InComponent('U1')` matches primitives *owned by the footprint*, which a routed track is not. **Never set MAX to 0.0762 anywhere that covers power**: VCC1V0 is 367 mA and STACKUP's own 50 Ω inner width is 0.168–0.195 mm. Then add, in priority order above the default: switch nodes (NetL1_1/L2_1/L3_1) 0.30/0.40/0.60; VU, VCC3V3, VBATT, USB5V0, VCC1V0 at 0.25/0.50/1.50; VCC1V8 and the analog rails 0.20/0.30/1.00; USB pair 0.150 fixed; SDRAM 0.0762/0.130/0.200. **VCC1V0 is the one to get right**: 32.35 mm from L3-2 (14.075, 9.050) to U1-N10 (46.400, 10.400), and at the current 0.1 mm preferred on 1 oz outer that is 156 mΩ and **57 mV — 5.7% of a 1.0 V rail with a ±5% spec.** At 0.500 mm outer it is 11.5 mV. Both inner signal layers are 0.5 oz (COPTHICK 0.689 mil), so anything routed on L3/L4 needs ~2.8× the width for the same current; `board/STACKUP.md` does not exist under `Zulu_Altrium/` and the 1 oz-inner assumption in the brief is wrong.

**Grids — measured this matters, and the fix is counter-intuitive.** Board6 currently has SNAPGRIDSIZE 0.127 mm (5 mil) with snap enabled, and TRACKGRIDSIZE = VIAGRIDSIZE = 0.508 mm. A 3 mil trace has 0.00932 mm of play per side in a 0.275 mm ball gap. Measured worst centreline error across the 36 real ball gaps: **0.025 mm metric grid → 0.00007 mm (legal); 0.0127 mm → 0.00626 (legal); 1 mil → 0.01254 (violates); 5 mil → 0.06195 (violates).** The hazard is imperial-versus-metric, not coarse-versus-fine. Set a metric 0.025 mm snap grid and match the track and via grids. Going finer than 0.025 mm buys nothing and makes hand-routing 238 balls slower.

**Net classes — there are none.** Classes6 holds 12 component classes and zero net classes, and every rule you are about to write needs one. Create: SDRAM (the 39 shared signals), USB (the pair), POWER_HIGH (VU, VCC3V3, VBATT, USB5V0), POWER_CORE (VCC1V0, VCC1V8), XADC (VCCADC, GNDADC, AIN15_*, AIN16_*), FPGA_ESCAPE, HDR_CHAN (the 32 X2 CHAN/ANALOG nets), FT_JTAG, SD, FLASH.

**Differential pair.** `DifferentialPairs6/Data` is 0 bytes and "All Differential Pairs" has 0 members, so the one DiffPairsRouting rule — All/All, 10 mil gap, 15 mil width, MaxUncoupled 500 mil — can never bind to anything and USB_D_P/USB_D_N would route as two unrelated nets. Create the pair, then scope: width 0.150 / gap 0.150 on Top (Zdiff ≈ 95 Ω uncoated on the stored stack, ≈ 90 Ω with the 0.4 mil Dk 3.5 mask), Top-only RoutingLayers, matched length 0.15 mm, and **MaxUncoupled ~2 mm, not 1.0 mm** — the forced break-out from a 0.30 mm coupled pitch onto U2's pins 7/8 at 0.5 mm pitch is itself 1.5–2 mm uncoupled, and the only legal route cannot avoid it. Note the pair must run west of x 29.20 and enter from inside the pad ring through the ~1.03–1.23 mm corridor: nothing passes between LQFP lands (0.2201 mm against 0.2562 needed).

**Planes.** Leave PlaneClearance at **0.2500 mm** — it is the only value in the set above JLC's likely 0.20 mm floor. Accept that both GND planes will be perforated under U1: the perforation is driven by *signal* escape-via anti-pads that PlaneConnect does not govern, and at a 0.70 mm void on a 0.5 mm grid they merge. Design the cut rather than pretending it away — leave two deliberate 3-slot radial gaps per side in the escape array so a ≥1.5 mm plane bridge reaches the core, and note the core island stays bonded through its own dog-bone vias and the Top/Bottom pours. Separately, PolygonConnect's Relief spoke is 0.254 mm, wider than the 0.225 mm BGA land and the 0.240 mm 0201 lands — that is a starved thermal by construction on every under-die cap; set the spoke to 0.15 mm with a 0.20 mm air gap, or Direct for vias.

**Routing Layers / Routing Priority.** All 32 layers are currently TRUE (including MidLayer 3–30, which do not exist) and every net's priority is 0. **There is no layer-direction rule in Altium** — RoutingLayers is a per-layer enable; direction lives in the Situs setup and in a written convention. Scope RoutingLayers per class: USB → Top only; SDRAM → Top + L4 + Bottom, L3 off (you cannot write "Top only within 1 mm of a pad", and an L4-only rule makes all 78 end stubs illegal, since U1's lands are Top and U3's are Bottom); POWER → Top + Bottom, never L3/L4. Set priorities: USB 100, switch nodes 95, SDRAM-CLK 90, SDRAM 85, POWER 80, flash 60, SD 55, FT/JTAG 50, header 30.

**Housekeeping.** Add USB5V0 to the Supply Nets list (the other eight rails are declared). Loosen SilkToSilk and SilkToMask from 10 mil to 0.10–0.15 mm — all 41 remaining silk violations are pad-versus-text, the rule's scope is (IsPad),(All), and routing provably cannot change the class, so this is purely to stop 41 lines of noise sitting on top of your real DRC results. Set RoutingCorners min setback from 100 mil (2.540 mm) to 0.10 mm. **Do not run Fanout on U1** — leave the FanoutControl rules alone, since they are only read by a command you are not going to use.

## ROUTE-TIME STRATEGY

**Order.** (0) Rules, classes, pair, keep-outs, room. (1) U1's core power fan-in: all 46 core balls and the 15 under-die 0201s run short Bottom or Top links out to the annulus or past the land-field edge — *no via sits inside rings 0–2*. (2) Place the escape via lattice by hand or by script on the annulus grid (rings 3–5, 120 slots; budget **75–80 used**, not 48, because ring-1/ring-2 plane dog-bones and core power land there too), leaving 2–3 slot gaps per side as plane bridges. (3) USB pair, Top, no vias. (4) The three buck switch nodes and their input/output loops, Bottom, local, fat. (5) SDRAM's 39 as one class. (6) Flash. (7) FT/JTAG. (8) SD. (9) X2's 32 CHAN nets. (10) LEDs, BTN, EEPROM, charger status. (11) Power rails as deliberate copper. (12) Pour, stitch, DRC.

**The anti-failure rule.** Stagger, never stack. Five via columns at 0.39 mm pitch between x 39.665 and 41.5475; no more than four vias in one column within any 2 mm of y. Two vias 0.40 mm apart leave −0.156 mm for a trace, so a packed column is a wall on every layer at once, and at 0.40 mm pitch the 0.80 mm anti-pads merge into a continuous slot through both GND planes — severing the return path of every signal that crosses it. That is the second, independent reason not to rebuild the column.

**Layer discipline across the crossing.** L3 and L4 carry the east-west bus (127 lanes each); Bottom carries the U3-side approach and the stubs into the 54 pads; Top is BGA ring-0/ring-1 escape only. **Do not put east-west traffic on Top across the corridor** — U2's east pad row walls it over y 9.71..17.49.

**Sort in the pocket, not in the strip.** U3's inboard Bottom pocket is 9.98 × 20.8 mm and completely empty — 124 vertical lanes per layer. The north-row/south-row sort happens there. The strip offers 16 vertical lanes and 17 nets need the sort.

**Nets to plan by hand, not hand to the router.** (a) D14@A14, D15@A15 and SD-DAT3@A16 as three parallel Top lanes through the 0.70–0.73 mm corridor between U1's north land edge (16.5125) and U4's pad copper (17.2400) — three lanes, ~0.11 mm spare, and a 0.30 mm via land there leaves only 0.2475 mm beside it, so that corridor is either three traces or one via row, never both. (b) AIN15_N@G2 and AIN15_P@G3: fixed balls at y 13.400 whose passives R12, R13 and C36 sit *inside* the strip at y 2.55..4.15, so each makes a ~9.5 mm north-south run down the strip plus a layer change. Give them the two westernmost via columns at the strip's south end (y 4.2..6.8) and reserve two vertical lanes, or move R12/R13/C36 east out of x < 41.79. (c) The 19 west-bound nets that sit on U1's **east** face (CHAN6–12, CHAN27/28, the UART group, FT-PWREN#, BTN, LED0_R/G/B, LED1, LED2) — 61 of 117 signals escape into the wrong half-plane. Their route is U1's south corridor (5.2555 mm, clear on Top directly under U1 but pinched to 0.325 mm past X4 west of x 36.6) or L3/L4. Decide which before the SDRAM bus takes the space.

**East face is via-down-only.** The 1.2375 mm strip at x 51.2525..52.0100 holds roughly 47–52 staggered slots against 26 east escapes; C111, C114 and C115's GND pads block the full band for ~0.78 mm of y each, and U4 covers it north of y 17.165. There is no Top route past the twelve-cap 0805 farm (x 52.25..57.40, 0.300 mm streets, one trace each).

**Power is not free copper.** The Bottom corridor x 14.69..41.86, y 6.860..16.840 is 9.98 mm tall — but it is bounded by U3's own pad rows and is where all 54 SDRAM pins escape. Power needs ~3.8 mm of it (VCC1V0 1.0 + VCC3V3 1.5 + VCC1V8 0.3 + VU 0.3 + clearances); allocate that explicitly **first**, then hand the remaining ~6.2 mm (about 36 lanes) to the bus. VCC3V3 has 129 pads, no plane and no polygon, 80 of them on the Bottom spanning 60.05 × 19.55 mm — decide what the Bottom layer's job actually is before drawing anything, because a VCC3V3 pour, a GND pour and a signal lane are all currently promised the same copper.

**One band, three claimants.** The empty Bottom strip at y 18.1..20.5 is ~11.7 mm of via-legal width after U4, Q1, R4 and the C93/C97/C98 lands are subtracted, and it is the only clear Bottom crossing between U1's 18 north-face signals and X2's north row. Proposals to relocate C87/C88/C94 and the XADC island all target it. If you move the XADC filter there (13.16 mm → ~5 mm to the VCCADC ball, and it frees the 1.058 mm Top lane at x 51.10..52.16 that is U1's only east-face Top escape channel), do that *and nothing else*, and do it as one costed decision rather than three independent ones.

**Interstitial vias are legal and not manufacturable.** Measured this session: U1's lands are round, so a 0.30 mm via land at a four-ball interstice clears by 0.091053 mm against the 0.090 rule — 1.05 µm. Treat that as unavailable. Every core power ball and every under-die cap pad runs out to the annulus or past the land-field edge.

**Whether Altium's interactive router honours keep-out regions.** The batch DRC demonstrably ignores keep-out *tracks* (155 physical overlaps, zero reported) and honours *regions* (742C083, 8 reported). What the interactive router does with either is unverified, and recommendation 1 depends on it.

**Which rule kinds are enforced live but never batch-reported.** Design Rule Checker Options6 has 34 batch indices and 24 online ones, and they are not nested: 14 kinds are batch-only, and **4 are online-only** (indices 9, 11, 17, 51). Four rule kinds will stop your cursor and never appear in a report. Nobody has mapped the enumeration.

**The coated differential impedance.** 94.7 Ω uncoated from a closed-form Hammerstad-Jensen solve, ≈90 Ω with a flat −5% mask correction. That needs JLC's own calculator against their 6-layer 1.6 mm build, not a hand formula. The pair is 9.6–14 mm against a tr/6 critical length of 14.2 mm, so a few ohms will not hurt you — but do not claim 90 Ω until someone checks.

**Whether the 9.98 mm Bottom corridor survives contact with the SDRAM bus.** It is empty today and nobody has routed it. Power's 3.8 mm allocation and the bus's ~36 remaining lanes are an arithmetic plan, not a route.

**The ring-1 plane-ball dog-bone count.** The Top escape budget assumed each of 20 ring-1 power balls costs one ring-0 perimeter gap; many are contiguous GND runs that can be poured together and share one via, so the real number is probably 8–12 and every face gains slack. That is the difference between the west face sitting at 14.5/18 and at 14.5/16, and it will not be known until the fan-out is drawn.

**Real VCC1V0 current.** `docs/power_budget.md` builds 367 mA on datasheet maxima plus explicitly assumed dynamic terms. A Vivado `report_power` run could move it by 2×. The width conclusion survives either way (even 700 mA needs only 0.16 mm outer), but the IR number does not.

**U1's eight netless balls** (A2, A8, A10, B2, B8, B10, D1, D2 — all bank-216 GTP TX and reference-clock pins, correctly open per UG482). Whether the PcbDoc treats them as true no-connects or as no-net pads on the plane is unchecked; either way it only helps.
