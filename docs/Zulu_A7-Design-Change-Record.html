2 · Boot and storage

The original design hung the config flash and the microSD off one shared SPI bus. They are now completely separate, which is what makes both standalone boot and a running Linux root filesystem possible at the same time.

Config flash — U4, master SPI

On the FPGA's dedicated configuration pins, which are fixed silicon, not a routing choice: CCLK C11, FCS_B K19, D00 D18, D01 D19, D02 G18, D03 F18. Mode pins strap M[2:0] = 001 for Master SPI (UG470 Table 2-1), so the part boots from flash with no host attached.

Re-checked pad by pad against the Winbond datasheet, SOIC-8 208-mil package code S: 1 /CS, 2 DO (IO1), 3 /WP (IO2), 4 GND, 5 DI (IO0), 6 CLK, 7 /HOLD or /RESET (IO3), 8 VCC — all eight match, and the FPGA end lands on the pin whose own name says so (IO_L1P_T0_D00_MOSI_14 and the rest). The IQ suffix is doing real work: it ships with the Quad Enable bit set to 1, factory-fixed (datasheet §7.1 and §11.1), which is what x4 boot needs. The otherwise identical IM suffix ships QE = 0 and would not boot in x4 until someone programmed its status register. Do not let a purchasing substitution change that letter. R2's 4.7 k pack pulls /CS, IO2 and IO3 up to VCC3V3 — needed because PUDC_B is strapped high, so the FPGA's internal pull-ups are off through configuration, and the datasheet asks for the /CS one by name.

microSD — X3, native 4-bit SD

Its own CLK/CMD/DAT0-3 on ordinary user I/O. DAT1 and DAT2 came from two bank-34 prototyping-header balls, which is what cost X2 two channels. An inserted card can no longer drive the configuration bus while the FPGA is reading its bitstream.

Note this is the native SD bus, not "quad SPI" — SD cards have no such mode. SPI mode and 4-bit SD mode are different protocols.

4 · Clock

One 12MHz 25ppm (FPGA + FT2232HQ) active oscillator. Its single CMOS output forks through R24 and R25 into two nets — CLK-12M-FT to the FT2232HQ's OSCI and CLK-12M-FPGA to ball L17 — with OSCO left unconnected. The FT2232H's USB PLL requires exactly 12 MHz, which is why the 100 MHz part originally floated was dropped; the FPGA synthesises whatever it needs with an MMCM.

The block diagram used to draw this as a two-terminal passive crystal hanging off the bridge. It is a four-pin oscillator with one output and two destinations, and the diagram now shows that.

Both rails carry VCC1V0. VCCINT and VCCBRAM are distinct FPGA supplies, but this board feeds both from the same 1.0 V buck — normal for a part this size — so the drawing separates them while the net does not.

VCCO decoupling, per bank

UG483 Table 2-2 splits VCCO into a Bank 0 column and an all other banks (per bank) column, and p14 states the VCCO figures are quantity per I/O bank. For CPG236 / XC7A35T that reads 1× 47 µF for bank 0, and per other bank 1× 47 µF, 2× 4.7 µF, 4× 0.47 µF. Note 3 lets one 47 µF cover up to four banks on the same voltage, and banks 14/16/34/35 are all on 3.3 V — so the total is 2× 47, 8× 4.7, 16× 0.47, which is what the design carries.

6 · FPGA ball map

230 of the 238 balls carry a net. Every one of them exists in Xilinx's pinout file for this exact device and package, none is marked No-Connect, and no signal lands on a non-I/O ball. Bank distribution: bank 0 × 23, bank 14 × 59, bank 16 × 16, bank 216 × 5, bank 34 × 30, bank 35 × 26, no bank (GND) × 71.

The 8 balls that carry nothing are all GTP transmit and reference-clock pins in bank 216, correctly left open — the GTP receive pins are tied to GND per UG482 Table 5-4. Zero general-purpose HR I/O remain free. Every usable pin on the part is spoken for, which is why the microSD's DAT1/DAT2 had to come out of the prototyping header rather than from spare silicon.

Still worth a Vivado pass. This check proves each ball is real, is an I/O, and is in the bank shown. It does not prove the assignment is legal for its function — clock-capable (MRCC/SRCC) placement, VREF pins, and DQS byte-group rules for the SDRAM all need Vivado's I/O Planner to confirm.

7 · X2 prototyping header

X2 went from 40 pins to 37, in two steps, both paid for by the same constraint: the FPGA has zero free general I/O, so anything new has to come out of the header.

  • Two positions were CHAN0/CHAN1 until their balls (W2, V2) went to the microSD as DAT2/DAT1.
  • One position was CHAN28. Its ball (M3) now carries UART CTS#, which completed hardware flow control on the FT2232HQ link. M3 was the right one to give up: highest-numbered so the header stays contiguous, in bank 35 alongside RTS# (N1) and DTR# (P1), and an ordinary HR pin — the other bank-35 channels are MRCC (CHAN22/23) or DQS (CHAN26/27) and are worth more where they are.
  • The USB landing sits between pin 5 and pin 6 — the three positions at x=39.37, 36.83 and 34.29, between CHAN3 and CHAN4. 10.16 mm centre-to-centre, 8.64 mm clear against X1's 7.80 mm of copper and 7.50 mm of body, so it clears both neighbours by 0.42 mm. On the board that is X1 at (22.90, 13.97) rot R90, opening facing the x=25.40 edge.
  • Every pin keeps the signal it had. Pin 2 is still CHAN0, pin 5 still CHAN3, pin 6 still CHAN4, pin 13 still CHAN11 — the gate-to-pad map did not change. Only pads 2-5 moved, each +7.62 mm into the space the gap had been occupying; pads 1 and 6-18 did not move at all.
  • The bottom row was closed up and is contiguous — pins 19..37 from x=49.53 down to x=3.81, and the empty position is the board corner at x=1.27. Getting there moved three pins 2.54 mm: +5V, ANALOG-IO0 and ANALOG-IO1. Their pin numbers did not change; their positions did. Anything already built against the old positions — a socket, an adapter, a constraint file keyed to physical position — is now wrong by one position on those three pins.
  • The gap is reserved, not spare. It is kept clear for the edge-mounted micro-USB. As drawn, X1 needs 2.60 mm for its contacts and about 7.8 mm for its shell against 6.10 mm of clear space, so the board edge has to move out or a third position has to go before it drops in.
  • Channels are now CHAN0..CHAN27 on pins 2-13 and 19-34, contiguous. The old numbering ran 0-13 then 15-31 — there was never a CHAN14. GND is pin 16, and it is the only ground pin.
  • The board is now 0.800 × 2.750 in — 20.32 × 69.85 mm — and sized to a breadboard. X2's two rows were 0.900 in apart, which is columns B and I and leaves one free column each side. They are now 0.700 in apart: columns C and H, with A, B, I and J free to patch into. The edges sit half a pitch outside the pins, so the long edges land exactly halfway between columns B and C. 24 grid positions per row now instead of 21; 38 are fitted and 10 left bare, the three spare columns falling together at one end so that 7.62 mm of board carries no through-holes at all.
    Two of the numbers originally asked for do not survive contact with the geometry, and are worth recording. 0.74 in is too narrow to hold the pads: at 0.700 in pin spacing it leaves 0.508 mm from pin centre to edge, where the 1.016 mm drill with its 25 % ring needs 1.062, so copper would hang off the edge — even the smallest drill a 0.025 in post tolerates still needs 0.763 in. And 2.75 in is 27.5 row pitches, not a whole number of rows, and not the same board as “rows 1 to 24”, which is 2.400 in. 2.400 was built first and then lengthened to 2.750: the pin grid still stops at 24 columns, and the extra 8.89 mm carries no grid position at all, being reserved for the Pmod header and its signals. The Pmod stands across that strip, 5.08 mm wide against 8.89 available. The earlier plain 53.34 × 25.40 rectangle — which replaced a 25.40 × 50.80 board with a 5.94 × 1.22 mm mini-USB tab — is superseded by this.
  • Both the package outline in the schematic and the layer-20 dimension in zulu_a7.brd were updated, since the outline is the one thing meant to survive the re-layout. X1's board position follows the USB landing — see the bullet above.

9 · Fixed along the way

  • R2.B pulled PROGRAM_B down in the original design. Xilinx wants it pulled up; that leg is gone and PROGRAM_B reuses the RST# net.
  • ANALOG-IO0/1 used to reach two PIC ADC pins. They are now the FPGA's XADC auxiliary differential pair.
  • The FT2232HQ's EEPROM was the wrong organisation. 93LC56A is 256 × 8; DS_FT2232H p27 requires 16 bits wide and names the 93LC46B. An × 8 part would have left the bridge unable to read its configuration, so it would have enumerated on FTDI's default VID 0403 / PID 6010 with no serial number — working, but not the custom identity the EEPROM is there for. Now 93LC46BT-I/SN, 64 × 16. Nothing electrical moves: CS, CLK, DI, DO, VCC and VSS keep their pads and their nets, and only the two unconnected pins were renamed.
  • The EEPROM pull-ups were wrong in value, and one of them on the wrong node. All three were 4.7 k, and R97 pulled up EE-DATA — the Data-In line. DS_FT2232H p11 says of pin 61: “Connect directly to Data-In of the EEPROM and to Data-Out of the EEPROM via a 2.2 K resistor. Also, pull Data-Out of the EEPROM to VCC via a 10 K resistor for correct operation,” and the reference circuits on p28 and p50-53 draw three 10 k pull-ups: EECS, EECLK, and Data-Out — none on the Data-In line. R96 and R98 are now 10 k where they were; R97 is 10 k and moved to EE-DATA-DO. Its drop crosses the three signal runs without joining them, which is how FTDI draws it too.
  • U4's land was a 150/208-mil compromise; it is now IPC-7351B nominal for the 208. The pads were 2.921 mm long, running 1.87 mm past the lead heel and putting 1.21 mm of copper under the plastic body. Recomputed from the datasheet's own section 10.1 at density level B — Z 8.806, G 5.788, X 0.558 — giving a 1.51 × 0.56 pad 3.65 from the centreline: 0.455 past the toe, 0.405 inside the heel, and 0.255 clear of the body. The outer envelope barely moves (8.70 → 8.81), so this buys no board width; what it buys is 43 % less copper per pad, no copper under the body, and a routing channel between the pad rows that widens from 2.86 mm to 5.79 mm — enough to take traces or a via field under the chip. Renamed SOIC-8_208MIL, because it is no longer a land that takes either body: a 150-mil lead, with toes at ±3.00 and heels at ±2.50, would overhang the new pad by 0.105 mm and sit on nothing. The rename also guarantees the regenerated board takes this land rather than silently matching the stale xess copy of the old name.
  • The header clock was on a ball that cannot take a clock. Vivado's placer rejects it outright — DRC PLIO-9, an error, not a warning: “The following clock source has been LOCed to a N-Type CCIO : CHAN_CLK”. CHAN-CLK sat on R18 = IO_L14N_T2_SRCC_14, the N half of a clock-capable pair, and a single-ended clock input has to be on the P half. place_design refuses to run, so as drawn nobody could have built a design that clocks from the prototyping header. Swapped with SDRAM WE# on P18 = IO_L14P_T2_SRCC_14 — same bank, and WE# is a plain output that does not care which half of a pair it sits on. Only the two <connect> entries changed; the pins are drawn visible="pad", so the sheet picked up the new balls by itself. CLK-12M-FPGA on L17 = IO_L12P_T1_MRCC_14 was already right.
  • The whole file is back on the 1.27 mm grid. 1175 of 1818 wire endpoints and 298 of 468 instances sat off-grid, by many different offsets — U2 by (−0.33, +0.19), R96/R97/R98 by +0.12 in x, the C39/C40/C41 and L4/L5 group by nearly 0.6 in both axes, and the whole of the FPGA-connections sheet by varying amounts. Anything wired between two parts with different offsets needed a dogleg to close the gap, and there were 34 of them, from 0.013 to 0.19 mm. They are invisible at normal zoom, they render as small rings when you zoom in, and they made the file fragile: nudge one part in Fusion and its runs come away from the taps, which is what happened to the EEPROM block on two consecutive exports. Now 0 off-grid endpoints, 0 off-grid instances, 0 wires under 1 mm, across all seven sheets.
  • The “56 stray junction dots” this record used to report were a bug in the audit, not in the schematic. The rule — a dot belongs where E + 2T + pin ≥ 3 — was only ever evaluated at wire endpoints. A decoupling pin landing straight on a supply rail with no stub of its own has E = 0, so it was never considered and its perfectly correct dot was counted as stray. That one omission accounted for 55 of the 56. The real state was one missing dot and two stray, all on VCC1V0, and the re-grid resolved them. The audit and the validator were both fixed to enumerate wire ends, pin positions and existing dots.
  • Whole-file geometry pass: 0 missing junctions, 0 stray, on every sheet; no wire endpoint on a foreign net; no overlapping wires.
  • uservalue normalised across 17 devicesets, so part values display the manufacturer part number rather than the deviceset name.
  • JP3's board footprint corrected 2×2 → 2×3 with all existing routing preserved — the board had always been short two pads for a six-signal JTAG header.
  • Block diagram brought in line: separate flash and SD buses, the Pmod box, the 4-bit SD bus, the CHAN-I/O count, oscillator-not-crystal, the Artix-7 label, and 64 MB.

Open items — read before layout

1. The board file is still the original Spartan-6 layout. 65 elements against 213 parts, and X1's element now sits outside the outline because the tab it was mounted on is gone — expected, and it resolves on regeneration. Its 421 routed connections are all for the Spartan-6 and are worthless. The stackup is already declared four-layer — layerSetup (1*2*3*16), with Route2 and Route3 active — but only Top and Bottom carry copper, so nothing has ever been routed on the inner pair. The design rules are the old board's and will not do for the BGA: a via at msDrill 10 mil with a 25 % annular ring comes out 0.56 mm across, where the diagonal channel between four 0.5 mm-pitch balls offers a free radius of only 0.204 mm. Rebuild the board from the schematic rather than forward-annotating onto this one.
2. The ball assignments have been through Vivado. tools/vivado_io_check.py reads the ball map out of the schematic, writes a Verilog stub with one port per user I/O and both clock inputs through BUFGs, and runs synth → opt → place → DRC on xc7a35tcpg236-1. Placement is what enforces the I/O rules, and it now completes clean: 101 ports on exactly the balls drawn here, no bank/VCCO/IOSTANDARD conflict, no dual-purpose config-pin conflict under SPIx4, no VREF or DQS complaint. Re-run it after any change to the pinout. It caught one real error — see §9.
3. Two footprints carry derived geometry. X1's shell tabs need Molex's 1050170001_sd.pdf; X3's shield pads need Hirose's own PCB pattern. The contacts on both are exact. Each package carries a layer-51 note recording this, so the provenance travels with the file.
4. The board now has real margin. Usable area has two zones: where the header pads are, only the 16.256 mm channel between the rows is free; past the last pad there are no through-holes, so the full 20.32 is. That is 1205 mm² a side, 2409 across both, against 1983 mm² of placed parts — +426 mm², about 21 %. Earlier revisions applied the channel to the whole length and so undercounted the board: the 2.400 in version read as break-even when it was really about +65, which means deleting BTN1 bought comfort rather than rescuing the design. Both packing factors (× 1.70 for chip passives, × 1.15 for everything else) are assumptions, not measurements. See Zulu A7 Board Plan.html.
  • SDRAM CLK drives from a single FPGA pin; the original double-drove it from two in parallel. Add the second back if timing margin needs it.
  • Nothing else free on the FT2232HQ is worth an FPGA pin — DSR#/DCD#/RI# are modem-era, and the ACBUS/spare BDBUS pins would only duplicate what the prototyping header already gives you. Since the FPGA has no free I/O, every one of them would cost a channel.
  • U10 pin 6 is confirmed, not assumed. This was carried as unverified because no Microchip datasheet was thought to be in the project folder. It is — DS20001749 — and it says so twice on its own front page: “64 x 16-bit Organization ‘B’ Devices (no ORG)” and “ORG pin is NC on A/B devices”. So NC@2 on pin 6 is right. The same datasheet puts the LC part at 2.5–5.5 V and specifies its clock at 2 MHz down to VCC = 3.0 V, so FTDI's 1 Mbit/s at 3.0–3.6 V has margin. Still true that a 93LC46C substitution would need pin 6 tied to VCC to select × 16.
  • Dropping 2 Kbit → 1 Kbit costs descriptor space: 128 bytes now hold the VID, PID, serial number, string descriptors and the per-channel mode bits. A long custom product string can fill it. That is a programming-time limit in FT_PROG, not a wiring one.
  • Around 23 text notes overrun the drawn sheet borders — cosmetic, but they will look wrong when plotted.
