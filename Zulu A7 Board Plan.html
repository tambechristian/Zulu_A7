Zulu A7 Board Plan

Front and back placement on the 1.000 × 2.400 in board, drawn to scale from the footprints in zulu_a7.sch. Generated by tools/board_plan.py.

The parts fit with 195 mm² to spare, 8 %. The header rows leave a channel 21.336 mm tall running the full length, so the usable interior is 1303 mm² a side — not the 1548 mm² the board measures — or 2605 mm² across both. Raw footprints come to 1543 mm², which reads as 59 % coverage, but raw footprint area understates the job because parts need room between them.

The two packing factors are measured, not assumed: each part is sized by the box make_board.py’s tiler actually places it against — its layer-39 courtyard abutting its neighbour, and its copper holding 0.30 mm off foreign copper, whichever is wider on each axis.

This budget used to guess × 1.70 and × 1.15 and said so plainly. Those were wrong in both directions and cancelled, so the total came out within 1 mm² of the measured one — right by luck. What it left out was everything that is not a part: 373 mm² of FPGA escape corridors, stage-2 moat, stage-3 fan-out ring and connector shroud. That omission alone is the difference between the +309 mm² this page used to claim and the +195 it reports now.

And bulk area is a floor, not a promise. It assumes any square millimetre can hold any part, when in practice a part has to sit near what it serves and the interior is a 21.336 mm channel — thin enough that the leftovers come out as strips too narrow to take an 0402 sideways. At the current length 0 parts are parked for want of somewhere to go, which is 0 mm² of parts against a 195 mm² bulk shortfall: the difference is fragmentation. Break-even needs 2.40 in of length; leaving room for the parts actually parked needs about 2.40 in. Everything else about the placement below survives a change of length, because the only thing that moves is where the far end sits.

Widening is the better lever, and it is a cliff rather than a slope. The pin rows are 0.900 in apart and stay there — that is the breadboard interface, not a layout choice — so widening the board does not touch the channel. What it grows is the strip outside each row, today 0.51 mm and worth nothing because a 0402 on end needs 1.20 mm.

0.850 in buys almost nothing; 0.800 in is the first width that holds every part currently parked. Widening also helps the part of the board that is not placement: the stage-3 fan-out ring is capped at half-width 6.20 mm by the board edge, and its 103 vias at 0.390 pitch already fill 81 % of that perimeter, which is why the fan still has 191 trace-to-trace violations. A wider board lets the ring move out, and the perimeter grows with it.

The cost is real and is not area: the board would overhang each pin row by -0.050 in instead of 0.050, one extra 0.1 in tie-point column per side covered on whatever it plugs into. That is a call about the product, not about the layout.

What decided the placement

  • The back can only take low-profile parts. The board sits pin-down on a breadboard, so the only clearance underneath is the header's standoff. That fixes X1, X3, J1, JP3, BTN and the LEDs to the front, because on the back nobody could reach them. Worth measuring before committing: the only package height verifiable from a local datasheet is U4's SOIC-8 208-mil at A = 2.16 mm max, so the flash is the tallest thing on the back. UG475 gives the CPG236 drawing as vector art with no height in the text, so the FPGA's height is not established here — and now that it is on the front, it does not have to be.
  • The FPGA is on the front; its decoupling is under it on the back. 39 capacitors on the FPGA POWER sheet, 156 mm² of them, want to sit directly behind the ball field — but that only requires the two to be on opposite sides. It says nothing about which side each takes, and an earlier version of this plan wrongly read it as a reason to put the A35T on the back. Top is the right side for it: it is the part that dissipates, the part anyone will want to inspect or rework on a 238-ball BGA, and the back faces a breadboard a couple of millimetres away with no airflow. The back keeps a 17.8 × 15.3 mm window clear beneath it for nothing but those capacitors. On the old board the SDRAM sat in that shadow, which is precisely why the decoupling had nowhere to go.
  • The microSD had to rotate. Its layer-39 keepout is the card plus 21.55 mm of eject stroke, along the connector's own −y. The board is 20.32 mm across, so the card cannot come out over a long edge. X3 is turned to R270 so the card leaves over the -x end, overhanging that edge by 5.60 mm. R270 and not R90: the keepout runs 8.70 mm past the copper on whichever side the slot faces, and at R90 that side was +x — the stroke pointed into the board and through BTN, so a card could only have been inserted from the middle of the board. check_board.py now compares layer-39 keepouts against copper on the same side, which is what found it.
  • The Pmod went along the bottom of the channel. 15.24 × 5.08 mm will not fit beside anything tall, but it fits under the USB landing, where the top 5 mm of the channel is spoken for and the bottom is empty.
  • Q1 moved to the front; the regulator stayed on the back. Q1 is cheap — three parts — and it earns the move: the 12 MHz square wave now sits on the same side as both things it drives, the FPGA's clock ball and the FT2232's OSCI, so neither needs a via. U8 does not travel alone. Its whole network is 25 parts and 76 mm² of footprint, and putting it on the front pushed that side to about 74 % of its usable area against the back's 48. Thermally there was nothing to gain either way — an LTC3569 in QFN-20 sheds heat into board copper, not into air — so it is back where the room is.
    What is drawn on the back is the tight core only: U8, the three buck inductors on SW1/SW2/SW3 and the input capacitor, 25.5 mm² of footprint and about 34 placed. Those five parts have to be close, because SW1–SW3 are the highest di/dt nodes on the board and must not reach their inductors through vias. The other twenty — three feedback dividers, the enable, mode and PGOOD networks, the input diodes, 50 mm² and about 86 placed — want to be near but not tight, and spread into the space around it. The back is now the regulator core, then the SDRAM, then the decoupling window, in that order from the -x end; the window still covers the ball field.

Still to resolve

  • The remaining passives. 143 chip parts totalling 444 mm², of which the FPGA decoupling window absorbs about 156 mm². The rest have to fit in the gaps left above and below the blocks — roughly 2.3 mm of channel above the back-side ICs and the front pockets at x 10–16 and x 35–45. That is the tightest part of the job and it is not drawn here.
  • The SDRAM is 22.35 mm long in a 60.96 mm board, and it now sits directly opposite the FPGA rather than beside it — U3 ends at x 39.55 on the back and U1 starts at 41.00 on the front, 1.45 mm apart. Moving the A35T right of the FT2232 put the ball field over where the SDRAM used to be, so the two swapped: U3 took the decoupling window's old place and the window followed the balls. The 39-signal bus is the shorter for it, but it now crosses sides at the FPGA edge rather than running along one; check the package trace delays in vivado/zulu_a7_io.csv before length matching.
  • BTN1 is gone. The button, its 10 k series resistor R87 and its 10 k pull-down R88 were deleted to buy back area. The ball it freed, B18, is now CHAN28 on header pin 41 — the bottom-left corner position, which was the one slot left vacant when the bottom row was closed up and which continues the numbering 37 → 38. Every ball on the part is in use again. BTN keeps its circuit unchanged.
